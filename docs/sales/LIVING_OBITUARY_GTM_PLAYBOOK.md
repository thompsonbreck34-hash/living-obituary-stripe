## 0. The Core Truth

Komar sells to one buyer. Living Obituary sells to **two**, and they are not the same person.

| Track | Buyer | What they buy | What they get | What they pay | Who closes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Facility Adoption** | Hospice / memory care / palliative director | Zero-work legacy program for families | $10 per family, brand association, a tool that makes them look good | Nothing — the $10 is a gift *to* them | BDR → AE |
| **Family Conversion** | The family at the bedside | A newspaper obituary, Q&A, photo pages, film, PowerPoint | The story, in their hands, while there is still time | $99 ($10 already gifted) | Mostly self-serve after the code is live |

The **$10 gift is the wedge**, not a footnote. It turns a cold sales call into a donation pitch. That is why hospices say yes in one conversation instead of three.

**The BDR's singular job:** book quality discovery calls with facility decision-makers.  
**The AE's job:** close the facility, get the code live, hand the family side to the product.  
**The family's job:** pay $99 and walk through the questions. Nobody sells to them. The product does.

## 1. Purpose & Mission

This playbook is the complete reference for Living Obituary's outbound function. It is designed to be read alongside the BDR flowchart — every step in the flowchart points here for the deeper explanation, scripts, examples, and decision-making guidance.

**The BDR role is singular in focus: book quality introductory discovery calls for the Closing Team (Adam).**

The product is warm and loving by design. It connects families and creates beautiful moments among loved ones in the most difficult times. That is not marketing copy — it is the mechanism. Families who use it sit together, answer one question at a time, and leave with the story in their own hands. The facility that offers it looks like the place that cared enough to give families something to do.

## 4. The Two-Sided Motion — How It Actually Works

### Track A — Facility Adoption (B2B, BDR-owned)

1. **Identify** a facility with a bereavement or legacy gap (see Signal Engine).
2. **Research** the decision-maker: Executive Director, Bereavement Coordinator, VP of Patient Services, Director of Philanthropy.
3. **Reach out** with the $10 gift as the hook — not the product. "Your families leave with a story. We send you $10 for every one that does."
4. **Discovery call** — understand their current bereavement offering, what families ask for, what staff time looks like.
5. **Demo** — walk them through the product with the `test100` zero-charge code. Let them feel the family experience.
6. **Activate** — assign a unique facility code, map it in Stripe Connect, confirm the $10 transfer fires.
7. **Hand off** — the facility prints cards, families scan, the product runs itself.

### Track B — Family Conversion (B2C, product-owned)

This track has almost no human sales. The family:

1. Scans the card or types the URL.
2. Answers one question at a time on their phone.
3. Pays $99 (or uses a promo code).
4. Enters the facility code → $10 transfers to the facility's charity automatically.
5. Downloads the five files: newspaper obituary (PDF + Word), questions & answers, photo pages, ten-minute stills film, PowerPoint.

The BDR and AE never touch Track B except to make sure the code is live and the transfer works. If the code is live, the family side converts on its own.

**This is the structural advantage over every competitor.** Dignity Therapy requires a trained clinician for an hour. Living Obituary requires a phone and fifteen minutes of family time. The facility gets the credit. The family gets the story. The $10 moves on its own.

**How to work a result:**

### HubSpot CRM — Using Your Existing Database to Hunt

HubSpot is not just a place to log calls — it is one of the most valuable lead sources already owned. The best BDRs treat it as a daily hunting ground.

**The four pools to work in HubSpot:**

| **Pool** | **What it is** | **Priority** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closed-lost deals | Deals that reached proposal stage but did not close | High — they already knew the product, just did not sign |
| Demo booked, never sat | Contact booked a discovery call but ghosted | Very high — intent was there, life got in the way |
| Dormant active contacts | Contacts who engaged (opened emails, clicked links) but went cold | Medium — warm signal, just needs a new touch |
| Inbound leads gone cold | Someone reached out but never got followed up properly | High — they raised their hand first |

**Pool 1 — Closed-lost re-engagement**

- Find: HubSpot Deals → Stage = Closed Lost → sort by Close Date (start 6–18 months ago).
- Read the Close Reason and last Activity Note first.
- Re-approach angles:
  - Went with competitor → "A lot of the hospices we work with came to us after [competitor] couldn't scale the family experience."
  - Timing / not ready → "When we last spoke, timing wasn't right. A lot can change in a year."
  - Price / budget → "I know cost was a factor. We've sharpened the model — happy to share what facilities at your size actually see."
  - No decision / went dark → "Wasn't sure if this was still on your radar."

**Pool 2 — Demo booked, never sat (highest priority)**

- Find: Contacts → Meeting booked activity + no Meeting completed activity, last 12 months.
- Script: "Hey [Name] — we had a call on [date] that didn't happen. I didn't want to write you off without checking in. Is a family legacy program still something you're working through? Happy to put 15 minutes back on the calendar — no prep needed."

**Pool 3 — Dormant clients**

- Find: Contacts → Last Activity > 90 days ago + Lifecycle Stage = Lead or MQL.
- Segment by last activity: email open = low intent, link click = medium, reply = high.
- Script: "Hey [Name] — it's been a while. A lot changes in a few months. If a legacy offering for families is back on your radar, I'd love to catch up. Even just 10 minutes."

**Pool 4 — Inbound leads that fell through**

- Find: Contacts → Original Source = Direct Traffic or Form Submission + no Deal associated.
- Script: "Hi [Name] — I was going through our records and saw you reached out a while back. I don't think we did a great job of following up, and I wanted to fix that. Are you still exploring options for families? Happy to make this a quick call."

## 7. Outreach System

### Cold Calling Approach

**Tone matching:** Read the room on the first three seconds. If they pick up sharp and fast — "Yeah?" — mirror that directness. If they answer warm and slow — "This is Mike, how can I help you?" — ease in. Do not have one speed.

**Call structure:**
1. State your name and company — keep it short. "Hey [Name], this is Adam from Living Obituary."
2. Lead with the gift, not the product. "We send hospices $10 for every family that writes their story with us. No monthly fee, no staff work."
3. Ask one question. "Are you handling bereavement support in-house right now, or do families mostly go home with nothing?"
4. Listen. Whatever they say next tells you exactly where to go.
5. If they have nothing → "That's the gap we fill. Families get a newspaper obituary, photo pages, a film, a PowerPoint — all written together while there's still time."
6. If they have something → "How's that working? Any families asking for more?"
7. If they're not the right person → "Who owns bereavement or family support on your team? Would you be open to an intro?"

### Common Objections — Full Rebuttal Guide

**Category 1: We already have something**

| **Objection** | **What it really means** | **Rebuttal** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We already run a bereavement program." | They may be happy, or it may be a binder of pamphlets. | "That's great — a lot of the hospices we work with had something similar. I'm not asking you to replace it. I just want to understand if families are getting a story they can hold, or just information. Would 15 minutes be worth it to see the difference?" |
| "We use Dignity Therapy." | Clinician-led, resource-heavy, limited throughput. | "Dignity Therapy is powerful — and it needs a trained clinician for an hour per patient. Ours is the self-serve layer on top: families write it themselves, on their phone, in fifteen minutes. Same emotional outcome, zero staff time." |
| "We've been doing memory books for years." | Loyalty, not necessarily satisfaction. | "Totally understand — that kind of tradition has value. A lot of our best partners came from long-running memory book programs where families kept asking for something more permanent. Not saying that's you, but would you be open to a 20-minute look?" |

**Category 2: Cost and budget**

| **Objection** | **What it really means** | **Rebuttal** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We don't have budget." | Budget objections are often timing in disguise. | "When does your budget cycle reset? And is this a not-now or a not-this-year? I want to make sure I'm talking to you at the right time." |
| "The $99 is too much for families." | They haven't run the numbers on what families already spend. | "Most families spend $200–$400 on a funeral obituary and flowers. $99 gets them a newspaper-quality obituary, photo pages, a film, and a PowerPoint — and $10 goes back to your charity. Happy to share the breakdown." |
| "We can do it cheaper in-house." | In-house costs are underestimated. | "Have you run the full cost including staff time, printing, and the emotional load on your bereavement team? Most hospices who do end up surprised. I can share a framework." |

**Category 3: Timing and priority**

| **Objection** | **What it really means** | **Rebuttal** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "This is not a priority right now." | Something else is on fire. | "Understood — what is taking priority? Sometimes what's urgent and what's important are different. If families are leaving without a story, that might actually be connected." |
| "We are not evaluating right now." | No formal RFP, but pain doesn't wait. | "Most of the hospices we work with weren't actively evaluating until something broke — a family asked for something they couldn't provide, a survey flagged a gap. I'm not trying to create urgency, but is there anything in your current setup creating friction?" |
| "Call me back in 6 months." | Real brush-off or genuine timing? | "Happy to do that — what is changing in 6 months? I want to come back with the right conversation, not just check a box." |

**Category 4: Process and trust concerns**

| **Objection** | **What it really means** | **Rebuttal** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We have complex family situations." | They've been burned by tools that don't handle nuance. | "That's actually where we tend to shine. We handle blended families, estranged relatives, multiple caregivers — the questions adapt. Can you walk me through what complex looks like for you?" |
| "We've had bad experiences with vendors before." | Protecting themselves. Most important objection to get right. | "That's really helpful context. What happened? I want to know if we have the same vulnerabilities — and if we do, I'd rather tell you now." |
| "Is this HIPAA compliant?" | Legitimate concern. | "Great question. The family enters their own data on their own device — we never touch PHI. The facility only sees the code and the $10 transfer. Happy to send the security overview." |

**Category 5: Competitor comparisons**

| **Objection** | **What it really means** | **Rebuttal** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "We're looking at [Competitor]." | Buying signal — they ARE evaluating. | "Good — that means you're serious. Can I ask what specifically you're comparing? I want to make sure you're comparing the right things. A lot of hospices come to us after realizing the demo looked great but the family experience was different." |
| "[Competitor] is cheaper." | Maybe true, maybe not full-picture. | "They often quote low on base and make it up on staff time and limited output. What did their quote include for the family — just a PDF, or the full set? Happy to do a side-by-side." |

**Deal stage definitions:**

| **Stage** | **Definition — move here when...** | **What must be logged** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New Lead | Contact identified, not yet contacted | Source, ICP tier, date added |
| Contacted | At least one outreach attempt made | Date of first contact, channel used |
| Connected | Had a live conversation — even 2 minutes | Call notes, what they said, their situation |
| Discovery Scheduled | Discovery call on the calendar | Meeting date/time, who from closing team is on it |
| Discovery Completed | Discovery call happened | Full call notes, next steps, who owns next action |

| Proposal Sent | Formal proposal or partnership terms sent | Date sent, what was proposed, follow-up date |
| Code Activated | Facility code live in Stripe Connect, $10 transfer verified | Code, acct_, transfer confirmation |
| Closed Won | Partnership signed / code active | Close date, deal value, notes on what worked |
| Closed Lost | Prospect chose not to move forward | Close reason (required), competitor if known, reactivation date |

**Required fields — never leave these blank:**

- Contact: First name, last name, email, phone, title, company name, LinkedIn URL
- Company: Company name, website, industry, employee count, ICP tier (A+/B/C/D), facility type (hospice / memory care / palliative / funeral / other)
- Deal: Deal name (format: "[Facility] — [Channel]"), associated contact, associated company, deal stage, close date estimate, **facility code** (if issued), **Stripe acct_** (if mapped)
- Closed Lost: Close reason must be tagged — never leave it blank

**Lead Status properties (for sorting/filtering):**
1. Unqualified Lead/DQ – wrong fit, not ICP, or not a fit for the channel
2. Attempting Lead – BDR is actively prospecting
3. Qualified – good fit, right ICP, right channel

**Data integrity rule:** If it is not in HubSpot, it did not happen.

**Weekly CRM hygiene checklist:**
- Review all deals with no activity in 14+ days — advance or close them out
- Check for contacts with missing required fields — fill them in before end of week
- Re-stage any deals that moved but were not updated
- Review sequences — any contacts stuck on a step? Anyone replied and not removed?
- Log the weekly scorecard before Friday end of day

## 11. Prospect to Close — Handoff Flow & AE Collaboration

The BDR's job ends when the discovery call is handed to the AE — but "ends" does not mean "disappear." The best BDRs stay close to the deal all the way through.

**The 6-step handoff flow:**

| **Step** | **Detail** | **Who owns it** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Identify ICP account | Source from directories, LinkedIn, Apollo, HubSpot, hiring signals | BDR |
| 2. Research | Perplexity: growth signals, hiring, grants, leadership changes | BDR |
| 3. Personalized outreach | Email or LinkedIn — research hook, not a template | BDR |
| 4. Discovery conversation | Lead with the $10 gift, then the family experience | BDR |
| 5. Log full notes in HubSpot | Contact info, situation, objections, agreed next steps — before handoff | BDR |
| 6. Hand to AE | Warm intro email or Slack. BDR stays CC'd. | BDR + AE |

**What goes in the HubSpot handoff note (required):**

- Company name, contact name, title, direct line, email
- Current situation — do they have a bereavement program? What do families get today?
- Pain points surfaced — what are families asking for that they can't provide?
- Decision timeline — when are they looking to make a move?
- Decision maker — is this person the DM or are there others involved?
- Budget context — did they mention budget? Any cost sensitivity?
- Objections already handled — what did they push back on, and what was said?
- Warm context for the AE — any personal details that help build rapport
- **Facility code status** — has one been issued? Mapped in Stripe? Transfer verified?

**Handoff rule:** The AE should never have to ask "what did they say?" before getting on the call. If the notes are complete, the AE walks in warm.

**Working closely with the AE after handoff:**

- Stay CC'd on all initial AE outreach
- Weekly deal review (15 min, Mondays): deals moved, deals stalled, one ICP insight each
- Re-engagement after AE handoff goes dark — BDR re-enters as a second touch with a different angle
- After every discovery call, AE gives BDR one of three pieces of feedback: strong lead / weak lead / wrong person — so the BDR prospects better next time

## 16. AE Closing Engine — Step-by-Step SOP

Mapped to the Living Obituary Deals Pipeline in HubSpot. Owner: Adam. Each stage = one deal stage.

**Pipeline Overview — 12 Stages:**

| **#** | **HubSpot Stage** | **Owner** | **Exit Criteria** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New (BDR believes this is SQL) | BDR | BDR tags deal as SQL-ready; AE notified |
| 2 | Initial Qualification (SQL) | AE | AE accepts or rejects within 48 hrs via HubSpot Note |
| 3 | Discovery Call | AE | 3 outputs captured: quantified pain, decision process, 2+ stakeholders |
| 4 | Demo / Test Code Sent | AE + BDR | `test100` or facility code shared; prospect walked the product |
| 5 | Proposal Sent | AE | Partnership terms or pilot proposal delivered and presented |
| 6 | Negotiation | AE | Terms agreed; all major objections resolved |
| 7 | Verbal Confirmation | AE | Decision-maker says yes; intent to proceed confirmed |
| 8 | Code Activation | AE + Ops | Facility code created, mapped in Stripe Connect, $10 transfer verified |
| 9 | Contract / Partnership Signed | AE | MSA or partnership agreement executed (if applicable) |
| 10 | Onboarding | AE + Ops | Cards printed, staff briefed, first family code used |
| 11 | Active Partner | Account Mgmt | Steady-state; expansion and referral conversations active |
| 12 | Expansion / Referral | Account Mgmt | Additional facilities, channels, or referral partners engaged |

**Status stages (non-linear):** Stalled/On Hold, Stay in Touch — Lost This Deal, Unqualified, Closed Lost, Needs Research, Passed – For Now.

### Stage 3 — Discovery Call (the heart of it)

**Before the call:** Review handoff note. Do not ask the client to repeat what they told the BDR.

**On the call — Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-Payoff:**

- **Situation:** Current bereavement offering? What do families get today? Any legacy program?
- **Problem:** What are families asking for that you can't provide? What falls through the cracks?
- **Implication:** What does that cost — staff time, family satisfaction, survey scores, reputation?
- **Need-Payoff:** If families left with a real story they wrote together, what would that mean for your families and your team?

**After the call (within 30 min):** Log call notes. Confirm the 3 required outputs:
1. **Quantified pain:** "Families leave with a pamphlet. Staff spend 2 hours/week on bereavement calls that go nowhere. Survey scores dip on 'emotional support.'"
2. **Decision process + timeline:** "Executive Director + Board decide. Next board meeting is Q3."
3. **2+ stakeholders:** "Bereavement Coordinator (primary), ED (budget), Chaplain (influencer)."

Send recap email with agreed next steps and the `test100` code.

### Stage 4 — Demo / Test Code Sent

- Share `test100` (or a facility-specific code).
- Walk them through the product: one question at a time, on their phone, the family experience.
- Show the five files: newspaper obituary, Q&A, photo pages, film, PowerPoint.
- Show the $10 transfer firing in Stripe (sandbox or live, depending on stage).
- Capture: did they feel it? Did they see the gap it fills? Any concerns?

### Stage 8 — Code Activation (the moment it becomes real)

This is the Living Obituary equivalent of "Contract Signed." The code is live, the transfer works, the facility can hand cards to families.

1. Create the facility code in the admin tool.
2. Map it to the facility's Stripe `acct_` (Connect onboarding complete, payouts enabled).
3. Run a test charge with the code — confirm the $10 transfer fires.
4. Log in HubSpot: code, acct_, transfer confirmation, date activated.
5. Hand the facility their card template and onboarding guide.
6. First family to use it = the proof for every conversation after.
