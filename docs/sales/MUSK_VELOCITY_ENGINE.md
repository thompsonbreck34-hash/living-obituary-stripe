## 01 — First principles

**Question we actually ask:** Does this have to be written after they are gone, by a stranger, from scraps?

**Answer we keep:** No. The person is still here. Write it with them. One phone. Their words. Five files.

**Industry assumptions we drop:**
- Obituaries belong after death
- Staff must produce the legacy
- Families cannot run the tool
- Memory must live on a monthly avatar
- More products at the door equals more care

Until one facility is live and one family has the files, that is the whole company.

---

## 02 — Decision velocity

Time-box information. Decide. Act. Learn. Maximize the percentage of correct bets — do not wait for a perfect bet.

**Risk absorption:** when a tool or a junior is frozen on a cheap question ("will the fee clear?"), the founder makes the call and says go. Then read the ledger.

**Cheap bets:** coupon name, pamphlet fold, sequence day-3 subject line.

**Expensive bets:** a sentence on a print PDF in a chapel, a claim about a life, a feature that speaks in the dead person's voice.

Charter gate before "go": does this honor the person in the room?

---

## 03 — Flat information, locked lives

Inside the company: anyone can see pipeline, Stripe state, defects, why a decision was made. No lego-hoarding.

Outside the company: family files stay private. Flat internally is not public internally. PHI stays off our servers by design. The family types on their phone.

---

## 04 — Critical path — no second-grade soccer

**Manned until done:**

1. One signed Colorado facility with a decision-maker and a pamphlet code
2. Stripe Connect live — $10 transfer proven on a real charge
3. One family completes a sitting and downloads the files
4. That story is the only case study that matters

**Unmanned on purpose:** new channels, holograms, extra SKUs, homepage poetry, sales-stack theater, robot avatars.

SWAT rule: if it does not move 1–4, it waits. Domain owners stay on domain. Do not pull Builder into sales. Do not pull sales into kerning while the ED meeting is unbooked.

**End-of-day pass-down (three lines):**
- Done today toward the four items
- Blocked by
- Tomorrow's single move

---

## 05 — Aggressive milestones as a forcing function

Compress "national hospice network someday" into **this month: one facility, one transfer, one family.**

The calendar is not a prediction to families. It is a tool that surfaces long-lead items: who signs, Connect identity, cards in the building, insurance after first paid proof.

**Reward function (intermediate wins):** first `true` on facilities PUT, first live charge with code, first email with downloads, first PDF a family would hand a celebrant.

Drumbeat: weekly scoreboard from the GTM playbook. Not a company-wide restyle of the heart icon.

---

## 06 — Factory mindset

Each paid sitting is a manufactured unit.

**Takt:** questions saved → files built → card captured → webhook marks paid → mail + downloads → 30-day hold → delete draft.

**Pass-down fields:** paid Y/N, files delivered Y/N, gift sent / unassigned / reversed, facility code, failure mode.

**Defects already paid for (fix the line, do not add a SKU):** vanishing sittings, migrations on family click, header crashing the name, sandbox vs live coupons, chunk 404s.

The factory makes keepsakes. It does not mass-produce fake people.

---

## 07 — Vertical integration is a survival question

Integrate only if the company does not exist without it.

**Must own:** sitting flow, file generation, Stripe checkout + Connect gift, facility code map, the vault window.

**Must not own:** hospice operations, funeral directing, LifeLoop, VA VLM, Dignity Therapy clinicians, churches, robots.

Partner. Code. $10. Proof. Next building.

## 08 — Talent density

Few people. Mission-aligned. Proven under load.

- Founder owns product, charter, first facility relationship, money
- Sales help gets a company-owned HubSpot, not the company, until terms are on paper
- Builder / Grok are tools. They do not set doctrine
- A closer who exploits a dying room is a no, regardless of close rate

Extended real-work evaluation beats pedigree. A 3PL playbook is a template, not a personality.

## 09 — Data backbone

If it lives only in a text thread, it does not exist.

| Layer | What it holds |
|---|---|
| This repo | Charter, playbooks, why a decision was made |
| HubSpot | Every dial, objection, code issued |
| Stripe | Every dollar, transfer, reversal |
| Neon | Sittings that must survive a second phone |
| Cloudflare secrets | Keys — never in chat, never on a pamphlet |

LLMs query the *company* backbone. They do not train on family answers. Family answers leave after the hold unless the family later chooses a paid vault.

---

## 10 — Mission alignment kills churn

Burnout here will not come from hours. It will come from rebuilding the app because files vanished, flipping gift math in public, sales theory with no building walked into, tools fighting each other.

Kill churn: one critical path, visible decisions, no information hoarding, weekly proof that a family is closer to holding the story.

The mission is not multiplanetary. It is: **no one's last true words die in a phone that never gets opened.**
